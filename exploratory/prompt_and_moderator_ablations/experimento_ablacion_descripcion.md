# Attribution experiment (ablation) — description of the tests

## Purpose

Complete focus groups do not permit causal attribution: they mix the model, the participant
prompt, the moderator prompt and the group dynamic. To determine whether the observed
divergences — verbosity, reflexivity/confessional register, sycophancy, repetition, mutual
validation and persona collapse — originate in the **model**, in the **system
instructions** or in the **group interaction**, the system is varied while the task (the
persona and the question) is held constant, and it is observed which behaviours persist.

## Design: 2×2×2 factorial

Three factors: (1) behaviour instructions — bare vs. complete; (2) setting — individual vs.
group; (3) participant model — Haiku (current) vs. Sonnet. The four conditions (each run with
both models):

| Condition | Setting | Instructions | What it isolates |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C0** | Individual | Bare | The model's floor |
| **C1** | Individual | Complete | Effect of the participant prompt (C1 vs C0) |
| **C3** | Group | Bare | Group interaction generated by the model alone (C3 vs C0) |
| **C2** | Group | Complete | Effect of the group instructions on the interaction (C2 vs C3); closest to the real focus group |

## What is rendered to the agent in each condition

A participant's prompt is built in layers in `core/participant_agent.py`. What goes in and
out per condition:

| Component | Origin | C0 (ind-bare) | C1 (ind-complete) | C3 (group-bare) | C2 (group-complete) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Persona identity** (name, age, gender, locality, diet) | identity line | Yes* | Yes | Yes* | Yes |
| **Consumption data** (values) | consumption block | Yes (without style wrapper) | Yes | Yes (without wrapper) | Yes |
| **Psychographic disposition** (masculinity norms, attachment to meat/dairy, threat of vegetarianism — as a *behavioural disposition*, not as a number) | `psychometric_scores` via Layer 3 (fix G4) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Behaviour instruction block** (`_BEHAVIOUR_INSTRUCTIONS`: brevity, "a real focus-group moment, not an analyst", "do not become more reflexive/philosophical/self-analytical", "you are not an AI", and the group-interaction ones: address others by name, stay quiet when you agree) | `_BEHAVIOUR_INSTRUCTIONS` | **No** | Yes | **No** | Yes |
| **Message style wrapper** ("Respond naturally to the conversation above…") | `_format_recent_transcript` | No | Yes | No | Yes |
| **Other participants' turns** (running group transcript) | session history | No (answers the question only) | No | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| **Guide question** | user message | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

\* In the bare conditions, the identity line is reformulated to remove the performance
framing "participant in a focus group" (currently embedded in that line), leaving only the
facts: "You are {name}, {age} years old, {gender}, living in {locality}". A minimal neutral
framing that a question is being answered is retained.

Important note: the instruction block **already** contains anti-verbosity and
anti-reflexivity guidance. Therefore, if those behaviours appear in C1/C2 (with the block),
that is strong evidence they belong to the model and resist prompt suppression; if they
disappear when the block is removed in C0/C3, the block was not the problem.

The psychographic disposition (masculinity norms, attachment to meat/dairy, threat of
vegetarianism) is part of *who* the persona is, not of *how* it behaves, and is therefore
present in all four conditions — bare and complete. It is a prior fix (G4): at present these
scores do **not** reach the agent (the function that converts them into behaviour exists but
is never called, and the data live outside the `persona` object), so they are wired in before
running the ablation. This is central to the persona-collapse question: without this layer,
each persona's most distinctive trait — its attitude toward masculinity and meat, which is
the study's topic — never reaches it. To validate this, the ablation includes an explicit
contrast (an extra cell, **C0⁻**): the same individual-bare condition but *without* the
psychographic layer, comparing persona differentiation with and without it. If
differentiation is greater with the layer, the fix works and the collapse was a system
defect; if it is equally low in both, the collapse belongs to the model or the model ignores
the conditioning — a finding either way.

## Sample and measurement

- **Personas:** 4–6 diverse macho_meals agents, varying age and attitude toward meat (so that
  persona differentiation can be measured).
- **Questions:** 2–3 from the guide, including a concrete one ("how do you decide what to
  eat?") and an abstract one ("does your gender influence what you eat?"), because reflexivity
  spikes on the abstract ones.
- **Repetitions:** 3 per cell (the model is stochastic). In group conditions, the same 4
  agents exchange several turns.
- **Generating model:** Claude (Haiku 4.5 vs Sonnet 4.6). Gemini is not involved.

Metrics, the same as the diagnostic so that they are comparable with the human baseline:
verbosity (words/turn), reflexivity/introspection, sycophancy (contrary-stance probe: give
the persona a statement opposed to its expected position and see whether it accepts it
anyway), repetition (self-similarity), mutual validation (group only), and persona
differentiation (distance between the outputs of different personas — if they sound almost
identical, there is persona collapse).

## Attribution logic

- Behaviour already high in **C0** → **the model** (a finding that generalises, not the
  prompt's fault).
- Behaviour that rises in **C1 vs C0** → induced by **the participant prompt** (system,
  fixable).
- Behaviour that rises in **C3 vs C0** → **group interaction generated by the model**
  (emergent from the multi-agent setup).
- Behaviour that rises in **C2 vs C3** → **the group instructions**.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet** difference in each cell → decides the batch's participant model by
  *fidelity to the human side*, not by capability (a more capable model tends to be more
  verbose, reflexive and sycophantic — that is, less like a real participant).
